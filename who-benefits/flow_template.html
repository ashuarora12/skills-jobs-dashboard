Chapter 05

How this was built, and its limits

Every number on this page is computed from public World Bank Group Scorecard exports by one Python script. Nothing is illustrative or simulated. The full methodology, robustness checks and annexes are in the written report (PDF, 22 pp.).

Data & provenance
  • Results and project records: World Bank Group Scorecard exports (scorecard.worldbank.org), FY25 cycle, results as of 30 June 2025. Covers health, nutrition & population services; WASH; gender-equality and economic-opportunity actions; financial services; and health-emergency capacity.
  • Context: Scorecard vision indicators for poverty at $3.00/day, food and nutrition insecurity, and basic hygiene. The latest year is shown for each region.
  • Cleaning: the export's “ACW” row is All countries, and the seven regions sum to it (checked in code). Only all-project rows are kept, because the disability-flag “Yes” rows are a subset. Results the export marks as double-counted are excluded, and exact duplicates across files are dropped.
Sourced vs. estimated
  • Sourced numbers come straight from the exports, or from simple arithmetic on them.
  • Model estimate numbers come from models fitted here. Each is shown with its validation or uncertainty.
  • Check reporting marks figures that look like reporting practice, not reality.
Limitations
  • Matching is not randomisation. Only observed differences are balanced, and unobserved ones (capacity, security) could still bias the comparison. Balance on a few covariates stays slightly above 0.1. The result is a matched association, not a causal effect.
  • Small informative sample. Only results where women are counted directly can show a gap, so the confidence interval is wide.
  • “Behind” is a straight-line benchmark. Results are back-loaded, so the flag is a screening signal, not a verdict.
  • The classifier is rule-based, and its recall is unmeasured. Precision was checked on small samples (5–12 per theme). Projects described in unexpected words will be missed.
  • Coverage. The Scorecard reports corporate results indicators, not every indicator a project tracks.
Privacy, fairness & reproducibility
  • Privacy: the analysis uses only public, aggregated, project-level data. No individual or household records.
  • Fairness: model performance is reported by region, income group and FCS status, and its weakest subgroup is named.
  • Validation: the classifier's review samples, labels and failed first version are saved in the repository. The review is a draft pending the author's confirmation.
  • Reproducibility: analysis/who-benefits/pipeline.py rebuilds every number with fixed seeds. Code & data on GitHub →
Related research
This connects to two strands of the author's work. The first is ongoing research on Global Skill Partnerships, skills and international labour mobility, which evaluates a skills-mobility pilot with propensity-score matching. The second is Bleeding at the Margins (Journal of Development Studies, 2023) on menstrual health among SC/ST women in India. Chapter 01 shows how rarely either topic appears among measured results.
